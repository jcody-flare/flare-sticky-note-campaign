Why it does not get repetitive

The premise and the invitation repeat. Nothing else does.

Two columns below are identical in every post. Two are entirely different. That is the whole structure, and it is what lets ten of these read as one campaign while staying tellable apart.

Note rows three and four: the same product, two different questions. Where can we grow this relationship, and which relationships do we need to protect. That is how the campaign shows range without becoming a feature tour.

The part that compounds

Every post ends by asking for theirs.

The product story earns the attention. Then the note comes back blank, turned toward the viewer, and asks the only question that matters to them.

That is a positioning statement and a conversation opener in the same four seconds, and a catalogue competitor cannot copy it, because for them it is not true.

“Every bank has a sticky note. Flare starts there.” … “What’s on your sticky note?”

Ten posts of this do not say Flare has a lot of banking products. They say Flare starts with problems worth solving.

Straight answers

What is still open.

No performance data
Nothing has been published. Every claim in this deck is about the creative, not about results. The first six weeks are what turn this into evidence.
One unverified number
LinkedIn draws its own controls over the bottom of a feed video. We have measured every frame against a conservative estimate of that area and all three clear it, but the estimate has never been checked on a real device. One private post and a screenshot settles it.
Post copy
The videos are finished. The written post above each one is drafted and wants a second pair of eyes before it goes out.
Order and cadence
Proposed: one a week, LeadIQ first. “Who should our bankers be calling?” is the most universally felt of the three and its story is the most symmetrical.
What a new post costs
These are rendered from code, not filmed. A new one is a script, one voice take and a build. No crew, no reshoot to correct a number. That is why a weekly cadence is a calendar question.
